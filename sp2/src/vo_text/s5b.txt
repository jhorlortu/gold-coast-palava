There is also the trap of success. The outsider became the establishment: he won the 1992 election with about sixty per cent, and when the opposition boycotted the parliamentary vote his party took almost every seat; in 1995, when crowds marched against the new V-A-T under the cry of KOO-meh PREH-koh, four people were shot dead by unidentified gunmen. He won again in 1996, with fifty-seven per cent against KOO-fwor's forty, and then, crucially, he obeyed the two-term limit. Here is the irony: on the seventh of January 2001 he handed power to John KOO-fwor, the first peaceful transfer from a sitting government to the opposition in our history, and KOO-fwor had once served about eight months as a secretary in Rawlings' own first P-N-D-C government, a move AH-hwoy says was partly about keeping Nigeria's President SHAH-gah-ree, a friend of KOO-fwor's, on side for oil.
