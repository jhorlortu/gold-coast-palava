*[B-roll: Accra street scenes, early 1980s; West German and New York archival; Heathrow, early 1980s. Typewriter family-tree graphic: "THE AGBOTUI SISTERS" → Rawlings' mother / Soussoudis's mother. Dateline: "ACCRA · 31 DECEMBER 1981".]*

**KOFI (V.O.):** Baaba, The Ascendant, wanted the man before the plane. Michael Agbotui Soussoudis was born in Accra in April 1946, to a Greek father and a mother who was one of the Agbotui sisters, the same family as Jerry Rawlings' mother, which made him the Chairman's first cousin, although the American newspapers kept calling him a nephew. He grew up in West Germany, went to college in New York, and kept American permanent residence. When his cousin shot his way back into power on the last day of 1981, Michael set off for home but stopped in London, sending emissaries and still more emissaries ahead of him because, as the exile magazine *Talking Drums* put it, he was not sure what reception his mercurial cousin would give him. By 1983 he was a fixture of Accra's party circuit, debonair, fluent in German, and working for the intelligence machine of Captain Kojo Tsikata.

**KOFI (V.O.):** Kwame, The Combative, refused to believe a Ghanaian could get inside the CIA. She met Michael, and they became lovers. That same year, a CIA security officer having dinner at her house noticed a photograph on her dressing table of a shirtless Michael with a blanket pulled up to his chest; she later said she had told her station chief about the relationship, and was told only to be careful.

Over about two years, the American indictment says, she gave him the identities of eleven informants and five CIA employees, the station chief among them, with cable traffic and details of a dissident plot to overthrow the government. The CIA's own internal study later recorded that she was the first known CIA employee to spy for an African intelligence service.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Even-handed checks built in:**
- Ghana's side: the penetration of the CIA and the bargaining down from ~400 to 8 are told as a professional intelligence result, in the words of the record.
- The cost: Scranage's prison term, the eight losing their citizenship, the families, and the unproven US claim of a killed informant.
- Ahwoi's account is credited to him every time ("Ahwoi says", "by Ahwoi's account", "in the words Ahwoi records").
- Awuakye: Ahwoi's memory of him ("When can I leave?") sits beside his own NRC testimony and the NRC's finding that stripping his nationality was illegal.
- Ahwoi's critics (Amidu; Rawlings' office) each get a line.

**Claims written with care on purpose:**
- **"Could possibly be" (counterintelligence ground one):** the dictated wording couldn't be checked against the page, so the script avoids the phrase and says only that the list "looked built to unsettle Ghana's own intelligence", which both readings support.
- **Arrest date:** 10 July 1985 (FBI-based reports and USA Today, "arrested Wednesday"). Graphic gives 11 July.
- **Swap date:** 23 vs 25 November in different sources, so the script says "late November 1985". Soussoudis's return (late Nov vs 3 Dec) is not dated.
- **Informant's death:** attributed to US officials (NYT, 13 Jul 1985) and flagged as never proven in public.
- **Coup plot:** only the indictment's wording ("a dissident plot to overthrow the government") is used. The arms-ship and Brazil story stays in the source notes, unverified.
- **Tsikata passing material to Cuba, Libya and East Germany:** a US allegation, so it's left out of the script.
- **The CIA station officer Ahwoi names ("Alan Roy") and his death:** not independently verified, so he's left unnamed in the script.
- **Scranage's time served:** sources differ (8 vs 18 months), so the script says only that she went to prison.
- **"Ahwoi was in the room":** he was one of the four negotiators by his own account. He did not travel to Lomé, and the script doesn't say he did.
- **David Queen timing:** the book gives no date. Press reports put Ahwoi's challenge to the Embassy in December 2003, during the NRC process, so the script says "in the early two thousands".

**Names to confirm (from dictation):** Awuakye; Dominic Adu-Gyamfi and the second Adu-Gyamfi's first name; Felix Peasah (middle name "Kotoku"?); Ellis Owusu-Fordjour (not spoken, notes only).
