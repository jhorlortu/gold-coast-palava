### 1. THE NEST (intro) · 0:00

*[Nest ambience, evening, a slow typewriter. Wide shot of the open newsroom. On the shared table: a large card map of Ghana and its neighbours, every border traced in glued string, red, blue and black.]*

**KOFI:** This evening the shared table at the Nest holds a map you cannot read with your eyes alone, because every border on it has been traced in string and glued down, red for Britain, blue for France, black for Germany; *[Close-up: a fingertip following a red string; we never see a face.]* and in our open newsroom, the whole team has watched one pair of hands move across it all afternoon, stopping again and again at the same town in the north-east, where the card underneath has been pressed thin. *[Close-up: the worn spot on the card. A pin label in Special Elite: BAWKU.]* That town is Bawku, and whose hands they are, I will tell you before we close.

My people, what dey go on? I am Kofi, the Editor, this is the Gold Coast Palava, and the price of this third edition, paid as always in data, is about fifty megabytes, a small price for a map Ghana has spent seventy years living with. *[PRICE STAMP on screen: ~50MB]* If you missed the first two editions, go back for them first, because the king who signed Asante into a colony signed the north into a protectorate on the very same day, and the cocoa farms of our second edition were worked, season after season, by hands from the very north this story is about. *[ON SCREEN: "Missed Editions 1 and 2? Watch them first." End-screen cards to Editions 1 and 2.]*

### 2. FRONT PAGE · 1:01

*[Five front-page cards slam in, one per question. The first is larger and stamped LEAD STORY; the last is a small boxed classified ad in the corner.]*

**KOFI:** Page one, and as the house rule goes, every headline is a question. Our lead: how did a king's capital end up inside a German colony while most of his kingdom stayed British, and what did a group of islands in the Pacific Ocean have to do with it? Which Gold Coast soldier fired the first shot of the First World War for the British Empire, and why was it fired in Togoland? Why did one territory vote in 1956 to join a country that did not yet exist, while four in ten said no? Why has a town in the Upper East slept under curfew since 2021? And tucked in the corner like a classified ad: which decision, taken by colonial officers almost a century ago, is still deciding who rules in Bawku tonight?

### 3. LEAD STORY · 1:44
**"Lines in pencil"**

**KOFI:** *[TIMELINE builds along the bottom of the screen as each date is spoken.]* Start in Berlin, where from November 1884 to February 1885 the powers of Europe agreed the rules for dividing Africa, with not one African in the room; *[TIMELINE: 1884–85 Berlin Conference]* Germany had already raised its flag east of the Volta that July and called it Togoland. *[TIMELINE: July 1884 Togoland]* Inland, Britain and Germany both wanted the trading country around Salaga and Yendi, so they declared it a neutral zone, which held only until the Germans marched on Yendi, and on the third and fourth of December 1896 the Dagbamba fought them at Adibo, the battle Dagbon still calls Adibo dali, the day of Adibo, before the Germans burned Yendi to the ground. *[TIMELINE: Dec 1896 Adibo dali] [SFX: distant fire, then silence]*

Then, on the fourteenth of November 1899, came a deal so strange it belongs on page one: in a treaty mostly about who would keep the islands of Samoa, on the other side of the world, *[MAP: a line from the Pacific to northern Ghana. Caption: "Samoa, 1899"]* Britain and Germany also split the neutral zone, so Yendi, the seat of the Yaa Naa, went to Germany, while Salaga and the western part of Dagbon stayed British. *[TIMELINE: 1899 Yendi to Germany]* On the twenty-sixth of September 1901, King Edward the Seventh signed Orders in Council on one day that made Asante a colony, the ending of our first edition, and created the Northern Territories of the Gold Coast, a protectorate in force from the first of January 1902. *[TIMELINE: 26 Sept 1901 / 1 Jan 1902 Northern Territories] [ON SCREEN: "See Edition 1"]*

The pencil moved again in 1914. Days after Britain declared war on Germany, the Gold Coast Regiment marched into Togoland, and on the seventh of August 1914, near Nuatja, a soldier named Alhaji Grunshi returned fire on a German-led police patrol, *[SFX: one rifle shot]* the first shot fired by a soldier in British service in the whole First World War, less than three weeks before the colony surrendered. *[TIMELINE: 7 Aug 1914 the first shot]* Britain and France then cut Togoland in two, and the new line put Yendi back with Dagbon on the British side but sliced the Ewe people in half. *[MAP: a red line drawn down through Togoland; Ewe areas shaded on both sides.]*

### 4. THE WIRE · 3:24
**"Wednesday, 9 May 1956, British Togoland"**

*[Dateline card in Special Elite. Grey, rain-season light. Ticker underneath.]*

**KOFI:** On Wednesday the ninth of May 1956, in the first weeks of the rainy season, when the farm roads around Ho and Kpandu turn to red mud, the people of British Togoland queued to decide their future under the eye of a United Nations commissioner, Eduardo Espinosa y Prieto of Mexico. Some one hundred and ninety-four thousand people were on the roll, and about eighty-three percent of them turned out. *[CARD: 194,230 registered · about 83% turnout]* The choice on the ballot was blunt: unite with the Gold Coast, racing towards independence, or remain a United Nations trust territory for now. Nkrumah's Convention People's Party campaigned for union; the Togoland Congress, strongest among the southern Ewe, campaigned against, dreaming of reunion with the Ewe across the French line.

When the count was done, ninety-three thousand and ninety-five people, fifty-eight percent, had chosen union, and sixty-seven thousand, four hundred and ninety-two, forty-two percent, had said no. *[RESULT CARD: Union 93,095 (58%) · Separation 67,492 (42%)]* The map tells it better than the total: the northern districts of Mamprusi, Dagomba and Gonja, together with Buem-Krachi, voted heavily for union, while the two southern Ewe districts, Ho and Kpandu, voted clearly against it. *[MAP: six districts fill in, four for union, Ho and Kpandu against.]* On the thirteenth of December 1956 the United Nations General Assembly approved the union, and on the sixth of March 1957 British Togoland was born with Ghana, the long eastern strip running from the Volta up through Oti and Dagbon.

### 5. THE PALAVA DESK · 4:35
**"Who holds the pen?"**

**KOFI:** Now travel north to Bawku, near the borders with Burkina Faso and Togo, where the question is not which country but which chief, and there are two histories here; this desk will not pretend there is only one. *[Split card, equal size, neutral colours: MAMPRUSI ACCOUNT / KUSASI ACCOUNT.]* The Mamprusi say their people founded the Bawku skin in the early seventeen hundreds and filled it for two centuries with chiefs sent by the Naayiri at Nalerigu; the Kusasi say they are the people of the land, its first settlers and its custodians, and that the colonial administration placed Mamprusi chiefs over them, which they date to 1932, because British indirect rule wanted kings to rule through, and the Kusasi had governed themselves without one.

Since independence, government after government has picked up the pen. *[A pen crosses out a name and writes another, four times, as each date is read.]* In 1958 the Court of Appeal ruled for the Kusasi; after the 1966 coup a military decree, NLCD 112, handed the skin to the Mamprusi; in 1983 PNDC Law 75 returned it to the Kusasi; and in 2003 a Mamprusi suit at the Supreme Court was withdrawn, which each side reads as its own victory. *[CARD: 1958 · 1966 · 1983 · 2003]* In February 2023 the Naayiri enskinned his own Bawku Naaba, Alhaji Seidu Abagre, at Nalerigu; when he arrived in Bawku in October 2024 the shooting returned, and by mid-February 2025 at least fifty-eight people had been killed, children among them. *[Black card, white text, no images of victims: "At least 58 killed, Oct 2024 to Feb 2025."]*

Asked to mediate, the Asantehene, Otumfuo Osei Tutu the Second, presented his report on the sixteenth of December 2025: it recognised the Kusasi chief, Asigri Abugrago Azoka the Second, as Bawku Naaba, and recommended that Alhaji Abagre return to Nalerigu or live in Bawku as a private citizen. The government accepted it and promised a one-billion-cedi fund to rebuild Bawku; the Naayiri rejected it entirely as flawed and biased; and on Christmas Day the army moved Alhaji Abagre out of Bawku. *[CARD: Report accepted by government · rejected by the Naayiri · GH¢1bn fund]* The ten-at-night curfew is still in force, and talks between the two communities, backed by the National Peace Council, go on. Chale, this desk gives no verdict; it only asks you to notice who was holding the pen each time. *[STAMP: "NO VERDICT"]*

### 6. THEN VS NOW · 6:23

**KOFI:** *[Split screen: 1956 ballot box / 2020 roadblock in the Volta.]* Then, forty-two percent of British Togoland voted to wait; now, a small movement says the wait never ended. On the sixteenth of November 2019, in Ho, the Homeland Study Group Foundation, led by Charles Kormi Kudzordzi, then in his eighties, declared a state of Western Togoland, arguing that the 1956 union was never properly formed; and at dawn on the twenty-fifth of September 2020, armed men linked to the movement raided the police stations at Mepe and Aveyime and blocked the roads into the Volta. *[TIMELINE: 16 Nov 2019 · 25 Sept 2020]* The state answers that a United Nations plebiscite settled the matter, the Volta chiefs disowned the declaration, and the courts have jailed members of the armed group; yet in May 2025 the ECOWAS Court ordered Ghana to pay seventy-five thousand dollars to thirty members held for long periods without trial. *[CARD: ECOWAS Court, May 2025 · US$75,000 · 30 detainees]* Both things are true at once.

It is not only our story: in 1947 Cyril Radcliffe, a British lawyer who had never seen India, was given about five weeks to divide it, and millions were uprooted. *[WORLD CARD: "The world: Partition of India, 1947"]*

And here is the item in the corner of page one. *[The corner classified ad from page one enlarges to full screen: THE 1%.]* Colonial rule drew lines inside the north too, deciding which chiefs would rule which peoples and writing it into law, so every government since has inherited disputes it could redraw but never erase. It also decided what the north was for: officials sent northern men south to the mines and cocoa farms and kept schools scarce, so the north's first secondary school opened in Tamale only in 1951, seventy-five years after Mfantsipim in Cape Coast. *[CARD: Mfantsipim, Cape Coast 1876 · Government Secondary School, Tamale 1951]* E no be small thing: the north was run as a labour reserve, and Bawku still lives inside a decision made for it nearly a century ago.

### 7. THE TEAM ASIDE · 7:54

*[Cut to the Nest. Amo does not speak; Kofi tells it. Hands and the map only; Amo's face stays out of shot.]*

**KOFI:** Back at the Nest, the hands on that string map belong to Amo, whom the Nest has decided to call The Discerning. He carries the name of Anton Wilhelm Amo, a boy taken from near Axim to Germany in the early seventeen hundreds who grew up to teach philosophy there, and like his namesake, our Amo does not take the world as it is handed to him. Amo is blind, and he learns a place by touch; his closest friend at the Nest, whose story is still to come, glued every string down for him, *[A second pair of hands, out of focus, pressing a string flat. No face.]* and he traced each line until his fingers knew Yendi by the knot where the 1899 string swerves away from it. Late in the evening he peeled the strings off, one by one, and laid his palm flat on the bare card. *[Close-up: a palm on plain card, strings coiled beside it.]* Kwame, The Combative, who had argued all day about whose line was right, ran his hand over it and went quiet, because under every string the card is one piece. I will tell you more about Amo in good time.

## Fact-check log (25 Nov 2026), verify before recording
- Berlin Conference, 15 Nov 1884 to 26 Feb 1885; no African delegates (standard). German protectorate over Togoland, July 1884 (Nachtigal treaties, standard; confirm the exact day if it goes on screen).
- Neutral zone around Salaga and Yendi agreed by Britain and Germany in the late 1880s (Kachim; Bening). Adibo, 3 and 4 Dec 1896; Yendi razed 4 Dec (Wikipedia, citing von Massow's diaries; Graphic; Ghana-Net uses "Adibo dali"). VERIFY: the Dagbani phrase and its pronunciation with a Dagomba speaker.
- 14 Nov 1899 Anglo-German convention partitioned the neutral zone, Yendi to Germany, Salaga to Britain (PRAAD ADM56/1/36, cited in the UFS journal), as part of the settlement over Samoa (Kennedy, The Samoan Tangle). "Mostly about Samoa" is the Editor's shorthand.
- Northern Territories, Ashanti and Gold Coast Orders in Council all dated 26 Sept 1901 (Hansard, HL Deb 16 Jan 1902; London Gazette, 1 Oct 1901); Northern Territories established 1 Jan 1902.
- Alhaji Grunshi, Gold Coast Regiment, 7 Aug 1914 near Nuatja (Notsé): the first shot by a soldier in British service in WWI (Cambridge; National Archives). Some sources give 12 Aug; the 7 Aug date is the more widely cited. Togoland surrendered 26 Aug 1914. The Australian Fort Nepean gun (5 Aug) is a rival claim for "first shot by the Empire", which is why the script says "by a soldier in British service".
- Plebiscite, Wed 9 May 1956: 194,230 registered, 160,587 voted (82.7%); union 93,095 (58%), separation 67,492 (42%) (UN A/C.4/334; FRUS; Hansard 25 July 1956). Mamprusi, Dagomba, Gonja and Buem-Krachi for union, Kpandu and Ho for separation (UN district table). Commissioner Eduardo Espinosa y Prieto (UN record). UNGA Resolution 1044 (XI), 13 Dec 1956. "First weeks of the rainy season, red mud" is a seasonal description, not a weather record for the day.
- Bawku: Mamprusi account (skin founded c. 1721 under Naa Ali, Mamprusi chiefs until 1957) and Kusasi account (autochthony, chiefs imposed under indirect rule, dated to 1932) (ModernGhana; Ghanaian Times; Wikipedia). 1958 Court of Appeal for Abugrago Azoka I; NLCD 112 (1966) for Mamprusi; PNDC Law 75 (21 Dec 1983) restored the Kusasi; 2003 Supreme Court suit withdrawn, read differently by each side (SOAS working paper; ModernGhana).
- Seidu Abagre enskinned at Nalerigu, Feb 2023; arrest warrant quashed 17 Oct 2024; arrived in Bawku late Oct 2024; at least 58 killed by mid-Feb 2025 (GhanaWeb, 15 Feb 2025). Curfew in place since 24 Nov 2021; 10pm to 5am in 2026 (Ministry of the Interior; Graphic, 24 Aug 2026).
- Asantehene's report presented 16 Dec 2025; recognised Asigri Abugrago Azoka II; Abagre to return to Nalerigu or stay as a private citizen; government endorsement and GH¢1bn fund, 17 Dec 2025 (Presidency; Graphic). Nayiri rejected it (Daily Guide; Sikaman Times, 17 Dec 2025). Military removed Abagre, 25 Dec 2025 (Graphic). National Peace Council talks with UNDP and Japan, Aug 2026. VERIFY just before recording that nothing major has changed in Bawku.
- Western Togoland: HSGF declaration at Ho, 16 Nov 2019, by Charles Kormi Kudzordzi, then 85 (Citi; Peace FM). Mepe and Aveyime raids and Volta roadblocks, 25 Sept 2020 (MyJoyOnline; GhanaWeb). WTRF founder jailed for five years (The Africa Report); Volta Regional House of Chiefs disowned the declaration (Graphic). ECOWAS Court, 16 May 2025, US$75,000 to 30 members (CounterVortex; GhanaWeb). The script does not name a party in connection with any group.
- Radcliffe: had never been to India; about five weeks to draw the line; line published 17 Aug 1947 (standard). "Millions uprooted" is conservative (estimates run to 10 to 20 million displaced).
- The 1%: forced and recruited labour from the north for southern mines and public works (Thomas, JAH; Cambridge Core article on labour in the Northern Province); education "rigidly controlled" in the north (MSU/Ghana Studies article). First secondary school in the north, Government Secondary School, Tamale, 1951 (Tamale SHS history; Graphic). Mfantsipim founded 1876.
