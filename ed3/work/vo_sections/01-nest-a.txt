This evening the shared table at the Nest holds a map you cannot read with your eyes alone, because every border on it has been traced in string and glued down, red for Britain, blue for France, black for Germany; and in our open newsroom, the whole team has watched one pair of hands move across it all afternoon, stopping again and again at the same town in the north-east, where the card underneath has been pressed thin. That town is Bawku, and whose hands they are, I will tell you before we close.

My people, what dey go on? I am Kofi, the Editor, this is the Gold Coast Palava, and the price of this third edition, paid as always in data, is about fifty megabytes, a small price for a map Ghana has spent seventy years living with. If you missed the first two editions, go back for them first, because the king who signed Asante into a colony signed the north into a protectorate on the very same day, and the cocoa farms of our second edition were worked, season after season, by hands from the very north this story is about.
