Now travel north to Bawku, near the borders with Burkina Faso and Togo, where the question is not which country but which chief, and there are two histories here; this desk will not pretend there is only one. The Mahm-proo-see say their people founded the Bawku skin in the early seventeen hundreds and filled it for two centuries with chiefs sent by the Nah-yee-ree at Nah-leh-ree-goo; the Koo-sah-see say they are the people of the land, its first settlers and its custodians, and that the colonial administration placed Mahm-proo-see chiefs over them, which they date to 1932, because British indirect rule wanted kings to rule through, and the Koo-sah-see had governed themselves without one.

Since independence, government after government has picked up the pen. In 1958 the Court of Appeal ruled for the Koo-sah-see; after the 1966 coup a military decree, NLCD 112, handed the skin to the Mahm-proo-see; in 1983 PNDC Law 75 returned it to the Koo-sah-see; and in 2003 a Mahm-proo-see suit at the Supreme Court was withdrawn, which each side reads as its own victory. In February 2023 the Nah-yee-ree enskinned his own Baw-koo Nah-bah, Al-hah-jee Say-doo Ah-bah-greh, at Nah-leh-ree-goo; when he arrived in Bawku in October 2024 the shooting returned, and by mid-February 2025 at least fifty-eight people had been killed, children among them.

Asked to mediate, the Ah-sahn-teh-heh-neh, Oh-toom-fwoh Oh-say Too-too the Second, presented his report on the sixteenth of December 2025: it recognised the Koo-sah-see chief, Ah-see-gree Ah-boo-grah-goh Ah-zoh-kah the Second, as Baw-koo Nah-bah, and recommended that Al-hah-jee Ah-bah-greh return to Nah-leh-ree-goo or live in Bawku as a private citizen. The government accepted it and promised a one-billion-cedi fund to rebuild Bawku; the Nah-yee-ree rejected it entirely as flawed and biased; and on Christmas Day the army moved Al-hah-jee Ah-bah-greh out of Bawku. The ten-at-night curfew is still in force, and talks between the two communities, backed by the National Peace Council, go on. Chale, this desk gives no verdict; it only asks you to notice who was holding the pen each time.
