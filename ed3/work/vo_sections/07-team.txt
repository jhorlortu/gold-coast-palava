Back at the Nest, the hands on that string map belong to Amo, whom the Nest has decided to call The Discerning. He carries the name of Anton Wilhelm Amo, a boy taken from near Axim to Germany in the early seventeen hundreds who grew up to teach philosophy there, and like his namesake, our Amo does not take the world as it is handed to him. Amo is blind, and he learns a place by touch; his closest friend at the Nest, whose story is still to come, glued every string down for him, and he traced each line until his fingers knew Yendi by the knot where the 1899 string swerves away from it. Late in the evening he peeled the strings off, one by one, and laid his palm flat on the bare card. Kwame, The Combative, who had argued all day about whose line was right, ran his hand over it and went quiet, because under every string the card is one piece. I will tell you more about Amo in good time.
